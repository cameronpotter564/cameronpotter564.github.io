Torsional Rigidity Jig

A test jig that twists a Formula SAE chassis to measure its torsional stiffness, so we can check our simulations against the real car.

When
Spring 2026
My role
Design and build, HyTech Racing
Tools
CATIA V6, TIG welding

Why it matters

A chassis that flexes too much changes how the suspension behaves, which hurts the car’s handling. We predict chassis stiffness in simulation, and this jig lets us measure it on the real frame to make sure those predictions hold up.

How the first version worked

  • Designed and built the jig for HTX, HyTech’s steel tube space frame car.
  • Both rear wheels were locked to the ground, and both front wheels were mounted to a beam that rotates about the chassis centerline. Twisting the beam applies a known torque, so we can measure how much the frame twists.

Results

The measured stiffness came out very close to our simulated value, which gave the team confidence in the chassis model.

What we learned and what’s next

During our design review, the competition judges pointed out that the setup was heavily overconstrained, which can make the chassis look stiffer than it really is. For HT11, HyTech’s first monocoque, I’m redesigning the jig to fix this. That work is in progress.
